    Raspberry Pi 4B GPIO pin map (car install)

    Status: design guidance, not hardware-validated. Nothing in this document has been tested on a physical Pi or in a vehicle. Requirement: REQ-HW-010 (software guard only); REQ-CAR-001 (ignition shutdown, tested with a mocked GPIO). Check every connection with a multimeter before powering the Pi.

    The Pi 4B GPIO is 3.3 V logic and is not 5 V tolerant. A 12 V car line (nominally 12-14.4 V, with load-dump spikes far higher) connected directly to a GPIO pin destroys the SoC.

    Software guard

    hardware/gpio_policy.py is enforced by the REST API (/gpio/configure, /gpio/set, /gpio/{pin}), by hardware/gpio_worker.py and by the simulation client.

    Result HTTP
    Pin or direction denied by the policy 403
    Malformed pin (not BCM 0-27), direction or value 422
    GPIO worker did not answer in 5 s 504
    GPIO worker answered with an error 502

    Override the default list with MIA_GPIO_POLICY_FILE (YAML) or MIA_GPIO_ALLOWED_PINS:

    # MIA_GPIO_POLICY_FILE=/etc/mia/gpio-policy.yaml
pins:
  4: [input, output]
  5: [input]

    MIA_GPIO_ALLOWED_PINS="4:io,5:i"   # i = input, o = output

    The file replaces the default list rather than extending it. An invalid override denies every pin and logs an error. The reserved pins below and any output on the ignition pin cannot be allowed by an override. Set the variables for both mia-api and mia-gpio-worker; the worker checks again.

    BCM pin roles

    BCM Physical Role Policy
    0, 1 27, 28 ID EEPROM (HAT identification) always denied
    2, 3 3, 5 I2C1 SDA/SCL: BME280, SHT30, ADS1115 (hardware/sensors_i2c.py) always denied
    7-11 26, 24, 21, 19, 23 SPI0 CE1, CE0, MISO, MOSI, SCLK always denied
    14, 15 8, 10 UART0 TXD/RXD (serial console) always denied
    17 11 Ignition sense (optocoupler), read by services/power_monitor.py input only
    4, 5, 6, 12, 13, 16, 18-27 except 17 various General purpose input and output

    BCM 18 doubles as PWM0 and PCM clock; BCM 12/13 as PWM. Keep them for general use only if no audio HAT or PWM device is attached. The default list is a starting point: trim it to the pins your harness actually uses via the override.

    Ignition on BCM17

    services/power_monitor.py configures BCM17 as an input with the internal pull-up and, on a stable edge to the ignition-off level (2 s debounce), publishes a shutdown notice, waits SHUTDOWN_DELAY and runs systemctl poweroff. Pin, delay and polarity come from IGNITION_GPIO_PIN, SHUTDOWN_DELAY and IGNITION_ON_LEVEL.

    Wiring (matches the power_monitor.py header):

    ACC 12 V --[1 kOhm]-- optocoupler LED+ ; LED- -- chassis GND
Pi 3V3 --[10 kOhm]-- BCM17 --- optocoupler collector ; emitter -- Pi GND

    A conducting optocoupler (ignition on) pulls BCM17 LOW; with the ignition off the pull-up holds it HIGH. The default IGNITION_ON_LEVEL=low therefore treats LOW as ignition on and fires on the rising edge. A driver stage that inverts the signal (ignition on = HIGH) needs IGNITION_ON_LEVEL=high, which fires on the falling edge. The unit tests check both polarities with a mocked GPIO; the real circuit is still to be bench-tested (REQ-CAR-001 keeps its not_proven).

    Use an optocoupler rated for the 12 V line with a series resistor sized for the LED current (a few mA), and a series diode or TVS on the ACC side against reverse polarity and transients. The optocoupler isolates the car ground from the Pi.

    Sensing 12 V

    Never connect a 12 V line to a GPIO. Two accepted ways:

    1. Divider plus ADC. A resistor divider (for example 33 kOhm / 10 kOhm, about 3.3 V at 14.2 V; recompute for your tolerance) feeding an ADS1115 input over I2C (ADS1115Sensor in hardware/sensors_i2c.py). The ADS1115 supply is 3.3 V so its inputs must never exceed 3.3 V: add a clamp (Zener or Schottky pair) at the input. Calibrate against a multimeter.
    2. Optocoupler for on/off signals, as for the ignition above.

    Relays and loads

    Drive loads through a relay or driver board (opto-isolated or with a transistor stage), never from a GPIO directly: a pin sources only a few mA and is 3.3 V.

    • Flyback protection across every inductive load (relay coil, solenoid, motor): a diode, or use a board that includes one.
    • A fuse on every 12 V branch sized for the wire, close to the source.
    • Power the Pi from a proper 12 V to 5 V converter rated for automotive transients (see Wiring and power), not from a GPIO or OBD pin 16.
    • Make outputs fail safe: choose the relay contact (NO or NC) so a Pi crash or poweroff leaves the load in its safe state. Boot-time pin states are undefined for a moment.

    Enabling mia-power-monitor

    mia-power-monitor runs systemctl poweroff on a stable BCM17 edge to the ignition-off level. With no optocoupler wired, the internal pull-up keeps the pin HIGH, but a floating or mis-wired input, a bad ground or an inverted stage can power the Pi off on its own, or never. Enable and start the service only once the ignition wiring exists and has been checked. tools/scripts/deploy-mia-rpi.sh leaves it off unless MIA_ENABLE_POWER_MONITOR=1; on a bench Pi without that wiring run sudo systemctl disable --now mia-power-monitor.
