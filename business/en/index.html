Your fleet, your data. Not the manufacturer's.

A new company car tells its manufacturer where and how you drive. MIA reads OBD and Audi diagnostics for you alone, runs on a Raspberry Pi under the seat and takes voice commands, so the driver keeps hold of the wheel. No cloud, and she writes nothing to the car.

  • Built on an Audi A4 Cabriolet 8H
  • Raspberry Pi 4B + ESP32 + Android
  • Read-only access to the vehicle

What works today

No slide deck. This is written and passes automated tests in CI or runs in simulation. Bench, hardware and in-car validation is still under way.

OBD-II telemetry

Engine speed, coolant temperature, battery voltage and ignition state, from an ELM327 digital twin and passive CAN capture on ESP32.

Read-only Audi diagnostics

A VAG/Audi diagnostics bridge that only reads. MIA does not write to the vehicle bus by default.

Number plate recognition

Plate recognition in the Android app; the Czech motorway vignette check runs on the Pi.

Voice commands

Voice command handling on the Pi and spoken feedback in the Android app.

Android companion app

BLE pairing, a live telemetry card and a local rules engine. Dashcam recording is still in development.

Raspberry Pi gateway

REST and WebSocket API, ZeroMQ messaging and systemd services, with simulation fallbacks when hardware is absent.

Evidence state of every requirement

Technology

Raspberry Pi 4B under the seat

The API, messaging and AI services run right in the car, not in someone else's data centre.

ESP32s behind the dash

They listen on OBD-II and CAN and drive local inputs and outputs. They write nothing to the control units.

Phone in the mount

Dashboard, BLE link and plate reading on the driver's phone. The dashcam is still being finished.

Cards on the table

Requirements, design decisions and validation records are public on GitHub. We hide nothing.
